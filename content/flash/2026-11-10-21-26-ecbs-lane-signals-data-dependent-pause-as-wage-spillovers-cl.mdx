## What Happened

ECB Governing Council member Colm Kelleher **Lane** signaled that future rate decisions will depend critically on evidence of wage and price spillovers, implying the central bank will not mechanically cut rates despite moderating headline inflation.

## What It Means

Lane's remarks inject **uncertainty into the rate-cut timeline**, delaying market expectations for aggressive easing and repricing the term structure of eurozone bonds. This extends the period of **elevated real rates**, which mechanically compresses net interest margins for **ING**, **BNP Paribas**, and **Santander** — pressuring near-term profitability but reducing refinancing pressure for bond-heavy balance sheets.

Simultaneously, higher-for-longer rate expectations lower the present value of long-duration cash flows, weighing on **growth-exposed equities** (tech, software) and **real estate investment trusts** (**Vonovia**, **Deutsche Wohnen**), while benefiting **utilities** and **defensive dividend stocks** that offer real yield protection. **ASML**, **SAP**, and luxury names like **LVMH** face multiple compression as cost-of-capital increases.

## Who Is Affected

Pension funds, insurance firms, and asset managers rebalancing away from equities into duration enjoy the repricing; corporates with floating-rate debt face higher refinancing costs. Retail investors holding growth positions face portfolio repricing; savers benefit from improved deposit rates and bond yields.

## What to Watch

Monitor next **euro-area wage data** (Q2 labour cost index) and ECB speakers through June; track the **Bund-BTP spread** for recession signals and **equity earnings revisions** downward for FY2024.
