## What Happened

The ECB raised its benchmark rate by **25 basis points to 2.5%**, citing escalating geopolitical tensions in the Middle East as a material inflation risk alongside core eurozone price pressures.

## What It Means

Higher rates compress net interest margins for **ING**, **BNP Paribas**, **Santander**, and **Deutsche Bank**, but strengthen their deposit franchises and loan pricing power—a mixed signal favoring large-cap European banks trading on dividend yield. Simultaneously, the **2.5% terminal rate signals an extended hiking cycle**, raising the present-value discount for long-duration growth stocks like **SAP**, **ASML**, and **Unilever**. REITs and utilities face margin compression as refinancing costs rise, pressuring **Vonovia**, **Deutsche Wohnen**, and **EDF**. The geopolitical inflation concern—energy price pass-through risk—supports defensive positioning in **Shell** and **TotalEnergies** as oil hedges, though near-term demand destruction tempers upside. Retail investors holding growth-heavy portfolios face mechanical re-rating; those in bond-heavy allocations see capital losses as duration extends.

## Who Is Affected

Pension funds, insurance firms, and asset managers managing ALM risk now face tighter reinvestment spreads; corporates refinancing debt face higher servicing costs. Consumers face delayed mortgage relief and higher borrowing costs, depressing discretionary retail spending and housing demand.
