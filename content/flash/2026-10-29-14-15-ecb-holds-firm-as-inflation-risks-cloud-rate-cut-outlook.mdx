## What Happened

The ECB left rates unchanged at its latest decision while signalling heightened inflation risks could delay or limit further cuts, reversing market expectations for a near-term easing cycle.

## What It Means

This hawkish hold reshapes the transmission across European equities in two directions. For **banks** — **ING**, **BNP Paribas**, **Santander**, and DAX lenders — a steeper, longer rate plateau protects net interest margins and deposit spreads, supporting capital returns. Conversely, higher-for-longer rates compress discount rates for **growth stocks** and **REITs**: **ASML**, **SAP**, and **Vonovia** face mechanical multiple compression as terminal rates rise. **Utilities** and **consumer staples** — traditionally rate-sensitive defensive plays — now offer less relative yield advantage, reducing their safety premium. The bond market reprices: **BTP-Bund spreads** widen as peripheral eurozone borrowing costs rise faster than core rates.

## Who Is Affected

Asset managers rotating out of growth into financials and value; pension funds and insurers recalibrating fixed-income duration and equity allocations. Retail savers benefit from higher deposit rates; mortgage borrowers and consumer finance users face extended affordability headwinds across France, Germany, and the Netherlands.

## What to Watch

Next ECB speaker signals and eurozone **CPI releases** in coming weeks; Q1 bank earnings (late April) will test margin guidance credibility under revised rate paths.
