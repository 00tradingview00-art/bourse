## What Happened

The ECB is preparing to discuss raising reserve requirements for eurozone banks at next week's governing council meeting, a move that would lock up additional capital on bank balance sheets.

## What It Means

A reserve requirement increase forces **ING**, **BNP Paribas**, **Deutsche Bank**, and **Santander** to hold more capital against deposits, directly compressing the loan-to-capital ratio and reducing lending capacity without proportional revenue gain. This mechanically tightens net interest margins already pressured by flat yield curves. Conversely, higher structural capital requirements reduce the discount rate applied to future cash flows for capital-light sectors: **ASML**, **SAP**, and **Unilever** see valuations re-rated upward as the hurdle rate for growth capex falls. REITs and Utilities face dual pressure—rising real borrowing costs for refinancing plus compressed bank lending appetite—while growth stocks benefit from lower cost-of-capital dynamics.

## Who Is Affected

European asset managers running core bank indices and insurance companies holding **AXA**, **Allianz** exposure face immediate mark-to-market losses; hedge funds betting on bank NIM recovery face reversal. Retail depositors and SMEs experience reduced credit availability as banks tighten lending standards to meet new capital floors.

## What to Watch

Monitor ECB President Lagarde's forward guidance on **May 2nd** and Q2 bank earnings seasons (late April–May) for revised NIM guidance; any hawkish inflation data before the decision would accelerate reserve hike odds.
