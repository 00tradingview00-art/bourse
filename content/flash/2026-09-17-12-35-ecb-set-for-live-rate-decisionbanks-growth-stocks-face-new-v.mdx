## What Happened

ECB Governing Council member Makhlouf confirmed the central bank will make a **"live" interest-rate decision** at its next meeting, signalling an active policy stance rather than a hold—implying either a hike or cut is under active consideration based on incoming economic data.

## What It Means

This signals the ECB is abandoning a data-dependent pause and preparing for **material rate action**. A rate hike compresses net interest margins (NIMs) for eurozone lenders **ING**, **BNP Paribas**, **Santander**, and **Deutsche Bank**, pressuring equity valuations despite near-term deposit stability. Conversely, a rate cut would lower the discount rate applied to terminal cash flows, mechanically repricing **growth stocks** (**SAP**, **ASML**, **LVMH**) upward by reducing the cost of future earnings. **REITs** and **Utilities** (dividend-sensitive sectors) face heightened volatility as their equity risk premia recalibrate with shifting real rates. Fixed-income investors face **BTP-Bund spread** uncertainty, particularly as Italian sovereign debt reacts to ECB policy shifts.

## Who Is Affected

Asset managers deploying capital across DAX, CAC 40, and AEX are repositioning duration and equity beta exposure; pension funds and insurers recalibrating liability hedges face immediate repricing. Retail savers and mortgage borrowers across the eurozone will experience direct transmission through lending rates within 4–6 weeks.

## What to Watch

The next **ECB policy meeting announcement and Lagarde's guidance** on inflation trajectory; concurrent **eurozone CPI print** will anchor market expectations for the magnitude of the move.
