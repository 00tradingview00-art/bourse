## What Happened

Eurozone composite PMI climbed to a **nine-month high**, signalling robust economic momentum and validating the ECB's shift toward a more restrictive policy stance, pressuring market expectations for a sustained rate hiking cycle.

## What It Means

This PMI surge reinforces the ECB's hawkish bias and extends the duration of elevated rates, triggering a fundamental revaluation of discount rates across European equities. **Banks** — **ING**, **BNP Paribas**, **Santander**, and **Deutsche Börse**-listed **Commerzbank** — benefit immediately as net interest margins widen with sticky-higher rates; deposit spreads remain favourable in a resilient demand environment. Conversely, growth stocks and **REITs** face headwinds: **ASML**, **SAP**, and **Unilever** see their free cash flow valuations compressed by higher cost-of-capital, while real estate vehicles (**Vonovia**, **LEG Immobilien** on Frankfurt) experience cap-rate expansion reducing asset values. **Utilities** and **Luxury** (LVMH) remain under pressure as consumer demand bifurcates toward essentials. **Cyclicals** and **Industrials** — automotive supply chains, machinery exporters — repriced higher on stronger demand signals and potential earnings revisions upward.

## Who Is Affected

Asset managers and pension funds holding overweight positions in growth and defensive sectors face immediate portfolio drag; ECB-sensitive bond traders now price in extended duration risk. European consumers and corporates face elevated borrowing costs, compressing discretionary spending and M&A activity.

## What to Watch

Next ECB Governing Council meeting and Q2 earnings revisions from **Santander**, **ING**, and **ASML** will confirm whether PMI momentum sustains rate-hike expectations or signals demand exhaustion.
