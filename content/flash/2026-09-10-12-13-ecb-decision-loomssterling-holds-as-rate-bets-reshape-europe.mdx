## What Happened

Sterling has held steady against the euro as markets price in the ECB's upcoming rate decision, with futures currently assigning **60%+ probability to a 25bp cut**, versus growing speculation around deeper easing in Q1 2025.

## What It Means

An ECB rate cut compresses **net interest margins (NIMs)** for eurozone lenders—**ING**, **BNP Paribas**, and **Santander**—by reducing the spread between deposit and lending rates, immediately pressuring Q4 earnings guidance. Simultaneously, lower policy rates mechanically lift **discount rates** for capital-intensive growth names like **SAP**, **ASML**, and **Siemens Energy**, repricing their future cash flows upward and supporting equity valuations. Utilities and REITs—**Unibail-Rodamco-Westfield**, **Deutsche Wohnen**, **Veolia**—also benefit from lower terminal rates, which reduce required yields and boost net present value of long-duration assets. The GBP/EUR stability signals the market has already digested the base case, reducing currency volatility for UK-listed multinationals on the **FTSE 100**.

## Who Is Affected

Asset allocators and pension funds managing eurozone equity and fixed-income portfolios face sector rotation headwinds: lower rates punish financials but support defensives and infrastructure. Retail investors holding bank equity funds or dividend-focused portfolios face margin compression; corporate borrowers and mortgage customers benefit from lower refinancing costs.

## What to Watch

Monitor the **ECB's December 19 policy announcement** and Chair Lagarde's forward guidance on 2025 cuts; any deviation toward **50bp or hawkish language** would reverse the current risk-on positioning.
