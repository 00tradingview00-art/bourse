## What Happened

ECB forward guidance now implies a potential rate increase cycle through 2026 before cuts resume in 2027, shifting market expectations away from the earlier consensus of cuts beginning in mid-2026.

## What It Means

This extension of the tightening cycle directly compresses net interest margins (NIMs) for eurozone lenders like **ING**, **BNP Paribas**, **Santander**, and **Deutsche Bank**—extending the drag on 2026 earnings before relief arrives. Simultaneously, the delayed pivot increases real discount rates for longer-dated cash flows, pressuring growth valuations in **SAP**, **ASML**, and other high-multiple tech names through 2026, while making cyclical and dividend-yielding sectors (banks, utilities, **Shell**) relatively more attractive in the near term. REITs face headwinds from sustained higher rates, but utilities benefit from the income appeal in a higher-for-longer regime.

## Who Is Affected

Long-only equity funds and pension managers holding overweight growth positions face tactical rebalancing pressure, while asset allocators shift into rate-sensitive value sectors. Retail investors in savings products see extended low returns on deposit rates, while corporates refinancing debt face higher borrowing costs through 2026.

## What to Watch

Monitor the December 2024 ECB policy decision and any January 2025 ECB speaker commentary for confirmation of the 2026 hike thesis. Q4 2024 eurozone inflation data and January PMI readings will be critical to validate whether the rate path holds.
