## What Happened

S&P Global's preliminary Purchasing Managers' Index releases for eurozone manufacturing and services today will reveal whether economic activity accelerated or contracted in the reporting month, with consensus expectations critical to cyclical equity repricing.

## What It Means

A PMI reading **above 50** (expansion) supports the narrative of post-ECB resilience and validates industrial earnings guidance; a **sub-50 contraction** reverses it instantly. This matters because manufacturing PMI feeds directly into valuation models for **Siemens**, **BASF**, **Airbus**, and **BMW** on the DAX, and **Renault** on the CAC 40—firms with earnings leverage to eurozone output growth. A beat lifts discount rates for cyclicals and near-term earnings revisions upward; a miss compresses both, triggering immediate fund reallocation toward defensives (utilities, healthcare) and weighing on industrials-heavy indices like the **DAX**. Services PMI equally signals consumer resilience, affecting European financial services exposure (**ING**, **Société Générale**).

## Who Is Affected

Macro hedge funds, asset allocators rebalancing eurozone equity exposure, and equity research teams revising FY2024–2025 earnings forecasts face binary trade execution. Retail investors in index-tracking ETFs and direct DAX/CAC 40 holdings absorb intraday volatility and portfolio mark-to-market swings.

## What to Watch

ECB speakers (Villeroy, Lagarde) on Thursday and eurozone unemployment data Friday will either validate or challenge today's PMI narrative, extending or reversing sector momentum.
