## Opening

European equities sold off broadly on Wednesday, with the DAX leading declines among major indices, falling 1.11% to 25,967 — the sharpest drop across the region and a signal that sentiment toward Germany's export-heavy economy remains fragile. The FTSE 100 shed 1.07% to 10,708.5, compounding pressure on sterling-denominated portfolios already navigating a softening currency backdrop. The euro slipped 0.23% against the dollar to 1.1596, narrowing the cushion that had supported European asset valuations for dollar-based investors in recent weeks.

Brent crude rose 1.94% to $92.25, adding to inflationary pressure on European energy-intensive sectors and raising input cost concerns for industrials. Gold's 1.34% decline to $4,421.60 signals reduced safe-haven demand, a shift that typically supports risk appetite in European equities but may prove fragile if crude prices sustain their advance.

## Key stock move

SAP fell 2.65% to €185.84, the sharpest decline among major European equities, dragging on the DAX as investors rotated out of the German software group amid broader technology sector selling. The stock's retreat weighed on sentiment across the index, where Siemens also slipped 1.59% to €281.05.
