## Introduction

The IBEX 35 is Spain's benchmark equity index, comprising the 35 most liquid companies listed on the Bolsa de Madrid and the broader Bolsa y Mercados Españoles (BME) exchange system. It serves as the primary reference for Spanish equities and functions as the underlying instrument for futures, options, and ETFs traded across European markets.

## What the IBEX 35 Contains

The index is a capitalisation-weighted, price index — meaning it does not account for dividends, unlike total return variants such as the IBEX 35 Total Return (ITOT). Constituents are selected and reviewed semi-annually by the Technical Advisory Committee of BME, which assesses liquidity thresholds based on trading volume over a six-month rolling window. A stock must typically rank among the 35 most traded by volume on the continuous market, with adjustments made for free float.

Current heavyweight constituents include Inditex (ITX.MC), which alone accounts for roughly 15–17% of index weight, making it the single largest drag or driver on any given session. Other major names include Banco Santander (SAN.MC), BBVA (BBVA.MC), Iberdrola (IBE.MC), and Cellnex Telecom (CLNX.MC). The index is notably concentrated in financials and utilities, which together represent close to 50% of total weight, creating meaningful sectoral skew for investors seeking diversified Spanish exposure.

The IBEX 35 is calculated in real time during continuous trading hours, from 09:00 to 17:35 CET, and disseminated by Sociedad de Bolsas, a subsidiary of BME, which was itself acquired by SIX Group in 2020.

## European Context

Under MiFID II, which came into full effect in January 2018, trading in IBEX 35 component stocks is subject to systematic internaliser and transparency obligations. Best execution requirements mean that retail orders in stocks like Telefónica (TEF.MC) or Repsol (REP.MC) must be routed through venues demonstrating competitive pricing — a rule that has accelerated the fragmentation of Spanish equity trading across multilateral trading facilities such as BATS Europe and Turquoise alongside BME's own Mercado Continuo.

ESMA's double volume cap mechanism, introduced under MiFID II, also periodically restricts dark pool trading in high-volume IBEX constituents, pushing more order flow into lit venues. For institutional investors, this has practical implications for execution strategy in less liquid IBEX names, particularly mid-weight stocks such as Fluidra (FDR.MC) or Solaria Energía (SLR.MC).

Spain's securities regulator, the CNMV (Comisión Nacional del Mercado de Valores), maintains oversight of issuer disclosure standards and short-selling thresholds — the latter being coordinated with ESMA under the EU Short Selling Regulation.

## Historical Episodes

During the European sovereign debt crisis, the IBEX 35 fell from approximately 11,000 points in early 2010 to a trough near 5,900 in June 2012, a decline of roughly 46%. Spanish bank stocks, particularly Bankia — which had floated in 2011 at €3.75 per share and collapsed to near zero within a year — epitomised the systemic stress that forced a €41 billion EU banking sector bailout for Spain in mid-2012.

In March 2020, the IBEX 35 dropped approximately 38% in six weeks as the COVID-19 pandemic triggered widespread lockdowns and Spain recorded one of Europe's highest per-capita infection rates. Tourism and real estate-linked stocks, including Meliá Hotels (MEL.MC) and Merlin Properties (MRL.MC), were among the worst affected, with Meliá losing over 60% of its value between February and March of that year.

More recently, the IBEX 35 outperformed major European peers in 2023, rising around 22% for the year, driven by strong earnings from Santander and BBVA and a resilient domestic economy that grew at roughly 2.5% — well above the eurozone average.

## What to Watch

Inditex earnings releases carry disproportionate index-level impact given the company's 15%+ weighting, making its quarterly updates effectively macro events for IBEX positioning.

Banco Santander and BBVA together represent over 20% of the index, meaning European Central Bank rate decisions and net interest margin trends directly shape index direction.

Spanish GDP growth and tourism data function as leading indicators for the consumption and real estate sub-sectors within the index.

The CNMV short-selling register, updated daily, provides a live signal of institutional bearish positioning in individual IBEX constituents.

Currency exposure matters even for eurozone investors: BBVA and Santander derive substantial revenues from Latin America, introducing BRL and MXN volatility into what appears to be a domestic index.

BME's semi-annual constituent reviews, typically in June and December, can trigger significant volume events as passive funds tracking the index rebalance.

The IBEX 35 lacks a dividends-included calculation in its headline form, so comparing raw index performance against total return versions or peer indices like the DAX — which is total return by default — produces misleading historical comparisons.
